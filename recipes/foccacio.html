What is foccacia

Focaccia is a flat, leavened, oven-baked Italian bread made from flour, water, yeast, olive oil, and salt. 

  • Flour
  • Sugar
  • salt
  • Yeast
  • Water

Step by Step Instructions

  1. Mix the Dough: In a large bowl, combine the flour, instant yeast, and salt. Add the lukewarm water and stir with a spatula or wooden spoon until a wet, sticky, shaggy dough forms and no dry flour remains.
  2. Add Oil and First Rise: Pour 1 tablespoon of olive oil into the bowl, turning the dough ball to coat it. Cover the bowl tightly and place it in the refrigerator to ferment overnight (18 to 48 hours) for the best flavor and texture, or let it rise at room temperature for 2 to 3 hours until doubled in size.
  3. Prepare the Pan: Pour 2 tablespoons of extra-virgin olive oil into a 9x13-inch metal baking pan or a similar rectangular baking dish.
  4. Transfer and Second Rise: Deflate the cold dough gently by releasing it from the sides of the bowl. Transfer the dough into the oiled pan. Turn the dough over once to coat it in the oil. Let it rise uncovered at room temperature for 2 to 4 hours until it spreads to fill the corners and looks very puffy and wobbly.
  5. Dimple and Top: Pour the remaining 1 to 2 tablespoons of olive oil over the top of the dough. Oil your fingers and press straight down into the dough all the way to the bottom of the pan to create deep dimples. Scatter flaky sea salt and fresh rosemary sprigs into the dimples.
  6. Cool and Serve: Remove the focaccia from the pan immediately and place it on a wire rack to cool for 10 minutes before slicing
